Key wins

What changed in the building this quarter

Faults found by continuous monitoring and fixed on the floor, with recovery verified in PEAK.

Evaporator pressure sensing restored on chillers CH-01 and CH-02

Both chillers had been carrying evaporator pressure faults, which leaves the plant running without reliable protection on its refrigerant circuit. On CH-02, Meridian Mechanical replaced the chilled water differential pressure sensor and confirmed the work on 21 July; on CH-01, Marcus Bell traced the reading back to a logic input on the chiller and corrected it on 13 July. PEAK has held both alerts recovered since.

Also visible in the equipment health snapshot: Chiller, up 5.00 pp across the quarter and into the Excellent band in August.

CH-02 — Inspect Evaporator Insufficient Pressure · CH-01 — Inspect Evaporator Pressure Broken Sensor

Supply air pressure reset now running on AC-02, AC-04 and AC-05

AC-02, AC-04 and AC-05 were each holding a fixed supply air pressure setpoint, so their fans worked as hard at quarter load as at full load. Meridian Mechanical implemented a demand-based reset strategy on all three, and the setpoints now step between 220–260 Pa and 300–320 Pa with load instead of sitting flat. Fan energy follows pressure closely, so this is the change most likely to show up on the bill; all three alerts recovered and closed by 24 June.

AC-02 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override · AC-04 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override · AC-05 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override

Failed plant controller replaced, returning AC-01 to automatic chilled water control

The chilled water valves on AC-01, AC-02 and AC-03 had been forced open by hand while a failed level 22 plant controller was on order, which meant the units were cooling flat out with no modulation. Meridian Mechanical replaced the controller and returned the valves to Auto, and PEAK verified AC-01 recovered on 30 April with the valve modulating normally again: a 22% average in the first day after the fix, rising to 49% by day seven.

Also visible in the equipment health snapshot: Air Handling Units, up 1.13 pp, the second largest gain on the page.

AC-01 — Inspect Chilled Water Valve Leak

AC-07 chilled water valve tuned out of a slow-response fault

AC-07 was delivering supply air 1.5–2 °C below setpoint because its chilled water valve modulated too slowly to keep up. Tom Whitfield range-tested the actuator to rule out a mechanical fault, then retuned the PID loop to match the other air handling units: integral from 0.3 to 3.0, proportional from 0.05 to 0.2, derivative to zero. The valve has modulated cleanly since, with no hunting in the change history, and the alert recovered on 12 May.

Notes on method

How these numbers are calculated

  1. Labour cost avoided. Each rule replaces a manual inspection at a set frequency by priority: P1 daily at 0.5 minutes per check; P2 weekly, P3 monthly and P4–5 quarterly at 1 minute. Savings prorate that annual effort over the 92-day period, valued at AUD 150/hr for this site's region.
  2. Thermal comfort score. The share of zone readings inside the ASHRAE comfort band during site working hours. The band is set per zone in PEAK and is 21–24.9 °C across this building, so a level scores 100% when every zone reading in working hours fell inside it. The site row comes from the site rollup and will not equal the average of the level rows.
  3. Zones in the indoor environment snapshot counts the thermal zones that returned a score over the window: 298 of the 314 zone temperature points in the analytics overview. The remaining 16 did not report often enough in working hours to be scored.
  4. Verified recovery. Recovery rate is alerts resolved in the period with fault status recovered, divided by all alerts resolved in the period, counting alerts with status closed on rules still running.
  5. Median time to resolve is measured from the linked action's creation to its resolution, not from the alert's own dates, since detection is automatic but resolution is human work.
  6. Raised vs resolved. Raised counts alerts triaged into actions in the month (or 1 where an action has no linked alert); resolved counts the same way on resolution date. Rules must be running; actions marked Not Doing are excluded from both series.
  7. The reporting window. Every monthly series closes on the last complete month, so no column or bar covers a part-month. Automated checks, labour cost avoided, and alerts raised and resolved can therefore be read against each other directly. The quarter itself is the three complete months named in the masthead.
  8. Completion rate is resolved ÷ (resolved + currently open) as at the issue date, so 100% reflects holding no open work.
