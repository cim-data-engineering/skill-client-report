Key wins

What changed in the building this quarter

Faults found by continuous monitoring and fixed on the floor, with recovery verified in PEAK.

Reliability

Evaporator pressure sensing restored on chillers CH-01 and CH-02

LevelB1

Both chillers had been carrying evaporator pressure faults, which leaves the plant running without reliable protection on its refrigerant circuit. On CH-02, Hawkins Air replaced the chilled water differential pressure sensor and confirmed the work on 21 July; on CH-01, Marcus Bell traced the reading back to a logic input on the chiller and corrected it on 13 July. PEAK has held both alerts recovered since.

CH-02 — Inspect Evaporator Insufficient Pressure · CH-01 — Inspect Evaporator Pressure Broken Sensor

Energy

Failed plant controller replaced, returning AC-01 to automatic chilled water control

LevelL22

The chilled water valves on AC-01, AC-02 and AC-03 had been forced open by hand while a failed level 22 plant controller was on order, which meant the units were cooling flat out with no modulation. Hawkins Air replaced the controller and returned the valves to Auto, and PEAK verified AC-01 recovered on 30 April with the valve modulating normally again: a 22% average in the first day after the fix, rising to 49% by day seven.

AC-01 — Inspect Chilled Water Valve Leak

Energy

Supply air pressure reset now running on AC-02, AC-04 and AC-05

LevelL22

AC-02, AC-04 and AC-05 were each holding a fixed supply air pressure setpoint, so their fans worked as hard at quarter load as at full load. Hawkins Air implemented a demand-based reset strategy on all three, and the setpoints now step between 220–260 Pa and 300–320 Pa with load instead of sitting flat. All three alerts recovered and closed by 24 June, with an estimated saving of $6,800.

AC-02 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override · AC-04 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override · AC-05 — Confirm Supply Pressure Override

Comfort

AC-07 chilled water valve tuned out of a slow-response fault

LevelL06

AC-07 was delivering supply air 1.5–2 °C below setpoint because its chilled water valve modulated too slowly to keep up. Tom Whitfield range-tested the actuator to rule out a mechanical fault, then retuned the PID loop to match the other air handling units: integral from 0.3 to 3.0, proportional from 0.05 to 0.2, derivative to zero. The valve has modulated cleanly since, with no hunting in the change history, and the alert recovered on 12 May.

AC-07 — Inspect Chilled Water Valve Leak

Notes on method

How these numbers are calculated

  1. Labour cost avoided. Assumes each rule replaces one manual check: P1 daily at 30 seconds; P2 weekly, P3 monthly and P4–5 quarterly at 1 minute. Prorated over the 92-day period at AUD 150/hr (regional rate) and a 7.6-hour working day.
  2. Thermal comfort score. Comfort band set per zone in PEAK, 21–24.9 °C across this building. The site row is PEAK's site-level score, not an average of the rows below.
  3. Zones. Zone temperature points configured for comfort scoring. Levels with no score in the period are excluded here and from the thermal zones figure in the analytics overview.
  4. Verified recovery. Share of alerts closed in the period with the fault recovered. Includes only alerts on active rules.
  5. Raised vs resolved. Counts actions, not alerts. One action can cover several alerts.
  6. Run hours. Drawn from run status or a speed, current or power reading. Enables and schedules are not used, as they show commanded state only. Where signals disagree, the lower runtime is used.
